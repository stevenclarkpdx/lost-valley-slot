# LOST VALLEY --- ART BIBLE

**Purpose:** Source of truth for all generated and hand-authored Lost
Valley visual assets.

## 1. Visual Identity

Lost Valley is a pulp paleontology expedition presented with the visual
language of a high-quality 1990s animated action-adventure series.

The intended feeling: - dangerous expedition into an unexplored
prehistoric world - field science mixed with adventure - dinosaurs
impressive and threatening rather than cute - graphic, energetic
casino-game presentation - premium modern readability without modern
glossy/mobile-game rendering

Directional visual language: - 1990s American Saturday-morning action
cartoons - dinosaur/adventure cartoons of that era -
comic-book-influenced television animation - bold hand-inked adventure
illustration - simplified cel-animation backgrounds

These are directional references, not instructions to reproduce any
specific show's characters or designs.

Do NOT use: - photorealism or 3D rendering - painterly fantasy concept
art - soft digital illustration - glossy mobile-game icons - chibi/cute
dinosaur art - pixel art - gritty desaturated realism - ornamental AI
micro-detail

Target shorthand: **A lost-world paleontology adventure drawn as a bold
1990s action cartoon, designed specifically for slot-machine
readability.**

Current refinement: generated symbols should look like objects drawn
for a premium 1990s animated action series, not realistically
illustrated objects with outlines applied afterward. This is a moderate
push toward animation-cel clarity, color, and graphic design, not a
redesign into parody or children's cartoon art.

Increase: moderate color saturation, separation between major color
regions, heavy expressive exterior ink contours, bold simplified
interior lines, hard-edged cel-shadow shapes, graphic highlight shapes,
warm/cool contrast, and visual punch at small slot-symbol size.

Reduce: realistic material rendering, subtle tonal transitions,
intermediate shades, fine surface texture, realistic reflections,
physically accurate metallic shading, muted/desaturated color, and small
representational detail.

Style test: ask, "Does this look like a prop that could have been
painted onto an animation cel in a serious 1990s dinosaur adventure
cartoon?" If it instead reads as a realistic object illustration,
concept art, product rendering, photograph, or material study converted
to comic style, it has not gone far enough toward the intended style.

## 2. Core Rendering Language

### Shape

Prefer strong silhouettes, chunky readable shapes, purposeful
exaggeration, clean curves, thick forms, clear foreground/background
separation, and simple planes rather than modeled surfaces. Slightly
oversized identifying features are encouraged when they improve
readability, while objects must remain believable and recognizable
rather than caricatured.

### Linework

Exterior contours use very strong, expressive dark ink lines. Major
interior forms use bold simplified dark lines. Minor detail is sparse
and should describe only important construction and form. Line weight
may feel hand-drawn but must remain consistent across the library.

At reduced game size, especially around 256 x 256 px, the dark exterior
contour and major internal divisions must remain immediately visible.
Decorative lines should disappear before the silhouette or major forms
become unclear.

Avoid hairline detail, excessive crosshatching, tiny cracks, noisy
texture, and soft painted edges.

### Shading

Use predominantly flat colors with limited cel-style shading: one
dominant local color, one broad shadow family, limited highlights, and
dark linework.

Prefer large hard-edged shadow shapes that feel intentionally designed
as graphic shapes rather than smooth changes in illumination. Highlights
should be selective, bold, and readable. Limited gradients are
acceptable for amber, sky, firelight, water, or atmospheric backgrounds,
but never as the dominant rendering technique.

Do not use airbrushed or smoothly modeled shading to create volume. Do
not rely on subtle tonal transitions, realistic reflections, physically
accurate metallic shading, or dozens of intermediate shades.

### Lighting

Lighting improves readability and drama rather than simulating perfect
materials. Prefer clear light direction, bold highlights, readable
shadow masses, and restrained rim light on premium objects.

Avoid bloom, lens flare, glossy plastic highlights, complex multi-source
lighting, and depth-of-field blur on symbols.

## 3. Color Language

Environmental families: jungle green, deep forest green, olive, ochre,
sandstone, warm earth brown, charcoal stone, slate gray, deep blue,
turquoise water, parchment cream.

Push saturation one moderate step above realistic expedition art. The
game should feel colorful and adventurous while avoiding both muddy
realism and neon children's-cartoon color. Prefer a small number of
deliberate color masses: a dominant local-color region, a darker
cel-shadow region, a brighter highlight/accent region, and very dark ink
outlines. This is not a rigid four-color limit, but avoid many subtly
different shades.

**Fossil Valley:** cool blue, turquoise, gray stone, pale fossil tones,
lush green accents. Mood: discovery, geology, ancient remains,
reliability.

**Predator Valley:** brick red, rust, dark crimson, burnt orange,
charcoal. Mood: danger, pursuit, violence, high risk.

**Nesting Grounds:** deep vegetation green, olive, moss, warm yellow,
eggshell cream. Mood: dense life, uncertainty, abundance.

**Amber:** rich orange, golden yellow, warm honey, burnt-orange shadow,
yellow-white highlights. Amber should immediately read as valuable.

High-tier discoveries may use stronger saturation and contrast while
remaining in the same world.

## 4. Reel Symbol Technical Standard

Unless the asset manifest overrides it: - master canvas: **512 × 512
px** - aspect ratio: **1:1** - output: **PNG** - runtime target:
readable at approximately **256 × 256 px** - every symbol in a
production set uses identical dimensions

Never generate rectangular reel-symbol masters.

### Safe area and scale

Keep the primary subject within approximately 82% of canvas width and
height. Nothing essential touches the edge. Normal subject occupancy is
roughly 70--80% of usable area.

Maintain consistent perceived visual weight. A compass should not appear
half the size of a canteen merely because their real-world sizes differ.

### Framing

Use centered objects and front, three-quarter, or simple profile views
selected for immediate recognition. Avoid unnecessary dramatic
foreshortening. Keep perspective broadly consistent across related
symbols.

### Background

Each reel symbol receives its own simple filled background unless
transparency is explicitly requested. Backgrounds reinforce category and
contrast while remaining subordinate to the object.

Suitable backgrounds include simple earth, rock, forest shapes, sky, map
texture, muted color fields, and compact expedition environments.

Do not build elaborate miniature scenes behind ordinary inventory
objects.

### Isolation

Every symbol is an independent asset. Never overlap neighboring assets,
allow another symbol into its background, crop the primary subject, bake
contact-sheet borders into the asset, or place text labels inside
ordinary reel symbols.

Contact sheets are review artifacts only.

## 5. Base-Game Symbol Hierarchy

### Utility / low-value

-   Mining Pick
-   Canteen
-   Compass
-   Excavation Brush

Use simple compositions, restrained backgrounds, strong silhouettes,
moderate saturation, and minimal dramatic lighting. Low value does not
mean low quality.

### Expedition

-   Jeep
-   Helicopter
-   Satellite Map
-   Scientist

These receive slightly more visual interest.

**Scientist:** adult female field scientist carrying or actively using a
notebook; competent researcher, practical field clothing, strong
silhouette. Avoid glamour-model styling.

**Satellite Map:** must immediately read as expedition/navigation
intelligence rather than a generic paper map.

### Evidence

-   T-Rex Tooth
-   Raptor Claw
-   Eggshell
-   Pterosaur Feather
-   Horn Fragment

Evidence should feel like meaningful scientific finds and have strongly
distinct silhouettes.

T-Rex Tooth: large, heavy fossilized theropod tooth. Raptor Claw:
strongly curved sickle claw. Eggshell: broken prehistoric eggshell
fragment, not a complete nest. Pterosaur Feather: use the game's
fictionalized pulp-science interpretation consistently; a rare preserved
feather-like specimen rather than a generic modern bird feather. Horn
Fragment: broken fossil horn section with clearly fractured base.

### Premium / special

-   Golden Amber
-   Fossil Valley Symbol
-   Predator Valley Symbol
-   Expedition Camp Wild

**Golden Amber:** rich golden-orange specimen with clearly visible
prehistoric insect inclusion. Luminous and valuable without glossy 3D
candy rendering.

**Fossil Valley Symbol:** round stone containing a recognizable
prehistoric fern/leaf fossil, on a cool blue background. The fossil must
not resemble cannabis or a generic modern decorative leaf.

**Predator Valley Symbol:** dinosaur footprint impressed into red/rust
rock, with a reddish background and dangerous visual treatment.

**Expedition Camp Wild:** compact expedition campsite in dense forest,
with visible tent/camp equipment and a strong campfire focal point. It
may be a compact scene but must remain readable at reel size.

## 6. Feature-Grid Symbols

Feature symbols use the same linework, cel shading, color philosophy,
and square geometry.

Required: - Small Fossil - Amber - Footprints - Fern - River - Nest -
Bone Cluster - Complete Skeleton - Living Specimen - New Species

Environmental symbols may use compact scenes where necessary.

**Small Fossil:** modest fossil embedded in stone; clearly low value.
**Amber:** follow the Golden Amber language, with value treatment
controlled by the manifest. **Footprints:** clear dinosaur tracks in
earth or stone; recognizable when reduced. **Fern:** prehistoric-looking
fern vegetation with a botanical fern silhouette; avoid cannabis-like
palmate leaves. **River:** compact lost-world river scene with
blue/turquoise water as the recognition cue. **Nest:** dinosaur nest
with multiple clearly visible eggs. **Bone Cluster:** excavated or
exposed group of fossil bones; more significant than Small Fossil but
less complete than Complete Skeleton. **Complete Skeleton:**
recognizable assembled or excavated dinosaur skeleton. Prioritize
skull/spine/body silhouette over dozens of equally weighted tiny bones.
**Living Specimen:** living dinosaur dominates the symbol, using a
dramatic clean pose and stronger contrast. **New Species:** highest
discovery tier. Use an unusual but believable dinosaur silhouette or
distinctive anatomical feature, premium framing, and the strongest
controlled contrast. Do not rely on text to communicate importance.

## 7. Characters and Dinosaurs

Humans use the same 1990s action-cartoon rendering: expressive, angular,
readable, and grounded in practical expedition clothing. Avoid pin-up
posing, photorealistic faces, excessive facial detail, and generic
tactical-military styling.

Dinosaurs should feel powerful, dangerous, and adventurous. Use bold
anatomy, clear species-defining silhouettes, expressive poses, strong
heads/jaws, and limited intentional skin patterning.

Avoid fantasy-dragon anatomy, arbitrary monster mutations, excessive
decorative spikes, hyper-detailed scales, and cute expressions.

Scientific plausibility is useful, but visual clarity and pulp-adventure
readability take priority over documentary reconstruction.

## 8. UI Art Language

UI belongs to the same visual world but is not constrained to square
geometry.

Useful materials include dark/worn metal, field notebooks, parchment,
canvas, leather, stone, wooden expedition crates, and jungle vegetation.

Keep framing relatively simple. Do not bury the interface beneath vines,
bolts, scratches, skulls, ropes, or decorative debris.

Functional hierarchy beats illustration. Important text and values
require quiet backgrounds, high contrast, sufficient padding, and
predictable framing.

### Feature Trigger Panel

Default concept: vertical dinosaur tracks leading away into dense
forest/jungle, strong depth direction, clear central path, darker
framing vegetation. Do not bake text into the reusable background.

### Field Notes Panel

Default concept: expedition field notebook, cream/parchment paper, dark
ink dinosaur skeleton/anatomy sketches, fossil observations, diagrams,
and handwritten-looking marks.

Generated pseudo-writing is decorative texture only. Player-readable
text must be rendered by the game. Leave deliberate quiet areas for
runtime text.

## 9. Typography

Generated art should generally not contain final gameplay text.

Titles, labels, jackpot values, buttons, instructions, and other
readable copy should preferably be rendered by the game for accuracy,
iteration, and localization.

Generated UI may include blank plaques, title areas, notebook lines,
decorative illegible handwriting, and empty labels.

## 10. Asset Families and Consistency

Generate and review assets in families: 1. Utility symbols 2. Expedition
symbols 3. Evidence symbols 4. Premium/base-game symbols 5. Feature
environmental symbols 6. Feature discovery symbols 7. UI panels 8.
Feature backgrounds 9. Frames/buttons/secondary UI 10. Effects and
animation-support assets

Do not generate the entire game as one image.

### Reference hierarchy

Visual authority, highest first: 1. approved Lost Valley assets 2. this
Art Bible 3. asset-specific manifest entry 4. generic textual style
references

Once approved assets exist, supply them as visual references whenever
the generation system supports conditioning. Lost Valley should
increasingly reference itself rather than continually reinterpret "1990s
cartoon."

## 11. Production Status and Versioning

Every asset has one status: - `planned` - `generated` - `review` -
`approved` - `rejected` - `superseded`

Only approved assets are canonical or shippable. Generation must never
automatically overwrite approved assets. Regeneration creates a new
candidate/version.

Use lowercase snake_case filenames:

`<family>_<subject>_<variant>_v###.png`

Examples: - `utility_compass_default_v001.png` -
`evidence_trex_tooth_default_v003.png` -
`premium_fossil_valley_default_v002.png` -
`feature_complete_skeleton_default_v004.png` -
`ui_feature_trigger_bg_v001.png`

Approved runtime copies may use stable names, while versioned masters
remain preserved.

## 12. Automated Validation

Before review, mechanically validate: - expected dimensions - square
aspect ratio for symbol classes - PNG format - valid alpha/channel
configuration - non-empty/non-corrupt file - filename matches manifest -
expected output path - no accidental alternate dimensions within a batch

Where image analysis is available, also flag: - subject touching canvas
edge - excessive empty space - obvious cropping - multiple unrelated
objects - unreadable silhouette - inappropriate text - style drift

Automated aesthetic validation is advisory. Human approval remains
authoritative.

## 13. Generation Architecture

Construct generation prompts from four layers:

**A. Immutable style block:** Lost Valley identity, 1990s action-cartoon
language, line/shading rules, anti-style rules.

**B. Asset-class block:** reel symbol, feature symbol, UI panel,
background, etc.

**C. Asset manifest:** subject, composition, palette, tier, dimensions,
special constraints.

**D. Approved reference assets:** whenever supported.

Do not create enormous unrelated bespoke prompts for every asset. Shared
rules belong in this Bible or reusable templates. Asset prompts describe
what is unique about that asset.

## 14. Common Failure Modes

Reject or regenerate assets showing:

**Style drift:** realism, painterly brushwork, glossy 3D materials, soft
children's-cartoon treatment, inconsistent line weight.

**Composition drift:** rectangular symbol canvas, undersized subject,
cropping, extreme camera angle, cluttered background, inconsistent
visual weight.

**AI clutter:** meaningless straps, buckles, bones, tools, foliage or
machinery; malformed equipment; duplicated parts; ornamental
micro-detail; nonsense text.

**Paleontology/readability failures:** fossil plant resembles cannabis;
tooth reads as horn/claw; raptor claw reads as ordinary talon; nest
reads as generic bird nest; skeleton becomes incoherent bone pile;
dinosaur becomes fantasy dragon.

**UI failures:** artwork consumes runtime-text space; fake generated
text presented as real information; ornament overwhelms controls;
inconsistent materials; important imagery crosses intended clipping
boundaries.

## 15. Review Criteria

Approve a candidate only if all are effectively true:

1.  It immediately reads as the intended subject.
2.  It remains readable at runtime size.
3.  It belongs beside existing approved Lost Valley assets.
4.  Its silhouette is strong.
5.  Its composition fits its asset class.
6.  Its visual hierarchy matches gameplay value.
7.  Its color identity is correct.
8.  Unnecessary detail is controlled.
9.  It is cleanly isolated.
10. It avoids obvious generative artifacts.
11. It would still look intentional after hundreds of spins.

An attractive image that fails these tests is not production-ready.

## 16. Guiding Principle

**Readability first. Consistency second. Style third. Detail last.**

Lost Valley should look deliberately illustrated, not generatively
impressive.

The best asset is the one that reads instantly, communicates its
gameplay role, survives reduction, matches the rest of the game, and can
be cleanly integrated and animated.
